Quick steps to make a free QR code menu

The fastest way to make a free QR code menu for your restaurant is to publish the menu online, copy the menu link, generate a QR code from that link, test it, then print it where guests can scan it.

  • Create a mobile-friendly menu page with categories, item names, prices, descriptions, and photos if needed.
  • Publish the menu to a stable public link.
  • Generate a QR code that points to that menu link.
  • Test the QR code on more than one phone before printing.
  • Place the QR code on tables, counters, windows, packaging, flyers, signs, or catering materials.
  • Keep the menu link live so you can update the menu without replacing every QR code.

QR menus work for more than restaurants

A QR menu is helpful anywhere customers need to see food or drink options quickly. Restaurants may place QR codes on tables, windows, host stands, or takeout menus. Cafes can put them near the register. Food trucks can add them to a window decal. Bars can use one for drinks, specials, and late-night food. Caterers and pop-ups can print QR codes on event signs or proposal packets.

The same idea also works for bakeries, juice bars, ice cream shops, breweries, tasting rooms, farmers market stands, hotel kitchens, poolside bars, and private chefs. If your menu changes, a QR code should point to a menu you can update without redesigning a sign.

Use a real menu page, not a hard-to-read image

Some businesses make a QR code that opens a photo of a printed menu. That works in a pinch, but it is often hard to read on a phone. A better QR menu uses a mobile-friendly page with readable text, tappable categories, current pricing, and clear item details.

What your QR menu should include

  • Business name, location context, and service details.
  • Clear categories for food, drinks, specials, desserts, or catering packages.
  • Prices and descriptions that are easy to read on a phone.
  • Photos where they help guests choose.
  • Sold-out or unavailable items removed quickly.
  • A printable version for guests who prefer paper or for events without reliable internet.

Where to put your menu QR code

Put the QR code where guests naturally pause. For dine-in, that may be table tents, check presenters, host stands, or patio signs. For counter service, try the register, pickup shelf, window, or line queue. For food trucks and pop-ups, use the serving window, sandwich board, or packaging. For catering, add the QR code to event signage and proposals.

Always include a short label such as "Scan for menu" and keep enough white space around the code. Print one test first, scan it under real lighting, and make sure guests do not have to zoom or guess what happens next.
